# Spark Landing Page — Design Context

## Product Context

**What Spark does:** Spark gives physical stores — starting with restaurants — the decision-making speed that online stores already have. It connects cameras, POS, and sensors into a live decision layer: when something goes wrong on the floor, Spark surfaces it in real time and routes the right action to the right person. Eventually, it routes those decisions to robots automatically.

**Core value prop:** E-commerce teams react to problems in seconds. Restaurant managers find out after the shift. Spark closes that gap.

**Three-phase product arc:**
- **Today** — Awareness: know the moment something goes wrong (table wait times, entrance queues, uncovered zones)
- **Next** — Action: the right person gets the right dispatch the moment it matters
- **Tomorrow** — Automation: hand those same calls off to robots, no human in the loop

**Live traction:** 32 Bahe Li Chaoshan Beef Hotpot locations in China. Managers use Spark to catch problems during the shift, not after.

**Target customer:** Restaurant chain operators running 10–50 locations — GMs, VP Ops, regional owners. Not enterprise IT, not individual restaurateurs.

**Team:** Bobby Yang (GTM, Los Angeles) + Longxin Peng (tech). YC Summer 2026 applicants.

**The bigger vision:** Restaurants are the first vertical. The same physical-vs-digital gap exists in retail, hospitality, fitness, healthcare. Spark's long-term bet: every physical experience eventually runs with the decision intelligence of an online one.

**Positioning:** Not a POS. Not a scheduling tool. Not a camera system. A live decision layer that sits on top of existing infrastructure. "The worker changes. The brain stays the same."

## Design Context

### Users
Restaurant chain operators — GMs, VP Ops, owners of 10–50 location regional groups. They're on the floor or just off it. They make fast decisions under pressure. They see technology through a skeptical lens: "does this actually work in a real restaurant?" They are NOT startup people, NOT enterprise IT buyers, NOT VC audiences.

Viewing context: desktop primarily (doing research), mobile secondarily (catching up between shifts). Business hours and evenings.

### Brand Personality
Three words: **urgent, grounded, forward**

- Urgent: physical stores move fast. The product responds in seconds. The brand should feel like it understands that pace.
- Grounded: not a startup pitch. Not AI hype. Grounded in the reality of a dining room floor — covers, shifts, sections, staff.
- Forward: Spark is ahead of where the industry is. It's bringing something new to a world that hasn't changed in decades.

### Emotional Goal
"This gets me — they understand my world." The operator should feel understood before they read a word. The design should signal: someone who knows restaurants built this. Not a tech company that decided to do restaurants.

### Anti-References
- **NOT**: Generic SaaS blue (Toast, Square, HotSchedules) — corporate, safe, forgettable
- **NOT**: Startup hype (gradient purple, glowing UI, "AI-powered" everywhere)
- **NOT**: Consumer-cute (pastel, rounded, friendly illustrations)

### Aesthetic Direction
Wise-inspired with a more physical, industrial edge. The lime green (#9fe870) and near-black (#0e0f0c) stay — they're distinctive and owned. What needs to evolve: typography should feel more like physical-world signage than a fintech app. The page should have the urgency of a well-run restaurant: efficient, no wasted motion, everything in its place.

Font direction: Billboard-weight condensed display type for headlines (Big Shoulders Display) paired with Inter for body — gives the physical/infrastructure quality that matches the restaurant world.

### Design Principles
1. **Every pixel earns its place** — no decorative elements that don't serve the message
2. **Physical first** — design choices should feel at home on a restaurant floor, not a startup pitch deck
3. **Speed as personality** — the page should feel fast. Tight type, clean sections, no fluff
4. **The operator's skepticism is the brief** — every section should answer "but does it actually work?"
5. **One idea per section** — restaurants work in zones. Each section owns one job
